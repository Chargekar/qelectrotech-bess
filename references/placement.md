# Placement conventions for engineering review

These are editable drafting conventions, not statutory dimensions or proof of PE approval. Select actual sheet size and the receiving agency's requirements. Keep code/product clearance dimensions separately sourced.

## Symbols and terminals

Use a consistent local grid and hotspot. Align circuit ports on straight orthogonal paths where possible. Keep visible device geometry distinct from terminal endpoints; terminal orientation should face the approaching circuit. Use consistent symbol families for the same function. Document whether a terminal represents an individual conductor or a circuit bundle.

Orient source-to-load reading consistently across related sheets. Direction of power flow is a separate annotation, especially for bidirectional BESS equipment. Do not flip a labelled symbol in a way that reverses positive/negative or line/load meaning. Use deliberate junctions and avoid ambiguous crossings. Keep labels away from device contacts, junction dots and conductor elbows.

## Annotation hierarchy

Place the stable equipment tag nearest its symbol. Put function/status beneath or adjacent, then concise verified ratings. Keep manufacturer/model detail in a coordinated equipment schedule when it crowds the drawing. Circuit IDs should sit along clear straight runs and map to a conductor/raceway schedule. Reference notes/details using unique keys; do not scatter repeated paragraphs over the SLD.

Reserve room for longer real model numbers and later redlines. Keep consistent text/leader alignment within a branch. Check the final print scale rather than judging a zoomed screen. Body text must remain readable without clipping, symbol interference or hairline strokes; tune font/line weights to the actual sheet and export path.

## Location and mounting drawings

Use declared physical units, origin, north and sheet scale. Link each equipment footprint to its SLD tag and product dimensions. Keep equipment footprint, door swing, service envelope, electrical working space and fire-exposure distances visually distinguishable. Dimension from stated surfaces/faces to actual obstructions, openings, boundaries and routes; do not infer distances from the schematic.

Show mounting elevation, substrate, pad/roof/wall support and reference the actual approved detail/calculation. A generic anchor or pad graphic is a drafting block, not an engineered anchorage. Show conduit path/risers/penetrations and transition details without implying unverified burial depth, support spacing, corrosion suitability or firestopping.

## Sheet conventions and issue control

Use an index, discipline/sheet number, title, scale/NTS, project identifier, revision/date, drawn/check/review fields and source/detail references. Keep the professional seal area blank for the responsible licensee. Distinguish existing, new, removed and relocated objects with a readable legend that survives monochrome printing. Track source revisions, clear superseded notes and preserve all open engineering questions in review issues.

Asset-gallery cards intentionally omit project ratings and connections. A clean card proves only that its generic geometry and labels render well; it cannot be represented as an engineered design detail.
